# Ordering effect versus a periodicity-only mechanism

October16, coordinator assessment of Qwen calls24–25 and the accepted body
constructor/diagnostics. Source reasoning only; no new experiment, dataset,
model, tolerance or frozen-protocol change. Whole model answers are rejected.

The frozen comparison can identify the total effect of its two assigned body
orders, given competence, correct pairing, fixed weights, equal horizon and
accepted uncertainty. It does not independently vary every feature of those
orders. Spectral lines, local motif reuse and higher-order counts are descriptors
of the intervention. Calling all of them pre-treatment covariates does not make
an adjusted contrast identify a periodicity-only mechanism. They may be fixed
before evaluating a model, but some are consequences of the chosen body arm.
Report realized descriptors; do not adjust away treatment-defining structure
and silently relabel the original estimand.

There is a simple design constraint in the current registered motif family.
For R>1, the periodic body is R copies of its final motif M. Its count of M at
registered motif boundaries is therefore R. A control with R motif slots and
that same count must put M in every slot, hence is exactly the periodic body
and violates the non-tiling control rule. Thus **exact matching of this particular
motif-exposure count is impossible within the current two-arm constructor**.
This refers to registered chunk boundaries, not all overlapping substrings.
R0/R1 are identical anchors. The observed descriptive count difference is a
structural feature of the design; it must not be portrayed as an independent
periodicity-only intervention or repaired by an invented tolerance.

A future discrimination could use a multi-motif superperiod and a control with
the same motif multiset but disrupted superperiod ordering, with context and
all other controls independently checked. That changes the treatment family
and requires a new reviewed preregistration; it is not an amendment to frozen
Gate B. It would still identify that new finite contrast, not a mechanism free
of every unmeasured higher-order feature. Causal state interventions and shams
remain necessary for the broader mechanism claim.

Qwen call24 usefully asks about motif-exposure alternatives, but its proposed
1% mismatch cutoff and unspecified spectral significance rule are unapproved
and scientifically unnecessary for exact design constraints. Its assertion that
wrapped input covariance/FFT measures required-history recoverability is false.
Those diagnostics characterize inputs; recoverability requires the separately
specified cue-conditioned complete-state/task measurements. Its statement that
a periodicity-only mechanism necessarily relies on local repetition is also
unsupported. No experiment or acceptance threshold is taken from this reply.

Qwen call25 correctly notes the logical gap between next-graph prediction and
prefix recall. However, it invents high next-graph accuracy in the untrained
calibration; the accepted calibration made no such competence finding. It also
switches to a vector GRU as if it established the native graph bridge and proposes
an unspecified nonlinear mutual-information calculation. These do not satisfy
native task or inference requirements. The valid logical possibility was already
in the charter: a representation sufficient for the next graph can omit an
arbitrary prefix, and low covariance alone need not mean harmful loss.

Keep native T-GRAB reconstruction as its own benchmark reproduction node.
A subsequent graph history intervention must actually require the varied prefix,
first establish competent writing/readout, and preserve complete H+C, graph and
training-seed sampling units, matched retention conditions and causal shams.
It must not be retroactively described as the original next-graph benchmark.
There is no accepted trained-memory or cross-modal result from these proposals.
