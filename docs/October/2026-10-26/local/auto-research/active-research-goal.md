# Active research goal

Activated at the owner's explicit request on **2026-10-16**. The session goal is
active; it is not achieved. No token budget, new compute allocation or model
provider entitlement is created by this goal.

Study representation collapse in temporal recurrent models, starting with
temporal graphs and extending to at least one native non-graph modality.
Produce reproducible theory, experiments and explanatory examples that establish
when task-required state distinctions are lost, what causes that loss, and when
memory survives. Demonstrate harmful collapse only where accepted evidence
supports it; retain valid negative results and preservation boundaries.

The governing novelty decisions are the
[novelty audit](../literature/novelty-review.md) and
[research charter](research-charter.md). The broad cross-modality umbrella is
not a result or a priority claim. The specific candidate contribution is:

> At fixed weights and equal retention horizon, does higher-order periodic
> distractor ordering cause excess loss of previously written, task-required
> information relative to matched non-periodic ordering? Through which
> state-transition mechanism, and under which conditions does stable attractor
> memory prevent the loss?

The matched-body contract controls counts, cyclic bigrams, run lengths,
start/end context, total updates, final motif, query and nuisance as specified
in the charter. Changes need a versioned protocol and review. Models must first
demonstrate competence and the task must actually require the varied history.
An ordinary next-graph score does not establish retention of an arbitrary cue.

Measure complete-state covariance geometry, finite-time tangent propagation,
native performance, recoverability and causal use separately. Count hidden and
cell state and all graph nodes in a fixed ordering. Periodic forcing alone does
not justify Floquet terminology: verify the trajectory and Jacobian periodicity
and closure first. Account for precision, noise, finite sampling, readout shifts,
nuisance compression, nonnormal amplification and categorical basin memory.
Global folding can erase required information despite full-rank local tangents
and unchanged pooled covariance; local contraction is not required for every
memory-loss mechanism. None of these single measurements proves harmful collapse.

The deliverable is an independently verified paper and artifact package:

1. Reconstruct the original temporal-graph baseline with pinned source/data,
   correct chronology, complete state and reliable checkpoint loading; label
   new training as prospective reconstruction when original lineage is absent.
2. Implement and validate the preregistered matched-order mechanism study with
   competence gates, independent training seeds, paired uncertainty and frozen
   confirmation statistics. Excess harm must be supported by the contrast at
   the named horizon; a positive difference-in-differences alone is insufficient.
   Preserve every failure and legitimate null.
3. Establish a predictive mechanism beyond the analytic nulls and named prior
   work; test causal corruption/rescue against matched shams. Predict memory
   preservation as well as loss. Correlation or a low-rank curve is insufficient.
4. Test transfer in a native non-graph temporal task. A renderer change of the
   same latent vector task does not establish transfer across modalities. More
   modalities follow evidence and measured compute needs.
5. Publish bounded claims, raw-result provenance, controls, uncertainty and
   limitations. Follow the charter's mechanism/null publication rules. Do not
   force a positive result, claim universal collapse or claim first use of Floquet
   analysis for recurrent memory.
